# 02 - Introduction to Celonis Apps

## What is a Celonis App?

A Celonis App is a packaged solution that brings together Views, Knowledge Models, and automation assets to help different users across an organization understand and improve their business processes.

Think of it like this:
- A **company** has many processes (ordering, invoicing, procurement)
- A **Celonis App** is a ready-made tool built specifically for one of those processes
- Different people (managers, analysts, operations teams) use different apps based on their role

**Real-world analogy:** Just like how your phone has different apps for different purposes (maps for navigation, calculator for math), Celonis has different apps for different business processes.

Examples of Celonis Apps:
- Duplicate Invoice Checker App
- Sustainable Spend Management App
- Unshipped Orders App

---

## Types of Celonis Apps:

### 1. Analytical Apps
- Used to **examine and interpret data** to understand how a process is performing
- Help you make **data-driven decisions** by identifying pain points
- Tools inside: filters, drill-down tables, Process Explorer, Variant Explorer
- **Interview answer:** "Analytical Apps help business users understand process performance through data visualization and exploration tools"

### 2. Operational Apps
- Used to **support and streamline day-to-day operations**
- Allow users to execute specific tasks or processes efficiently
- Built for specific use-case processes
- Examples: Duplicate Invoice Checker App, Open Order Processing App
- **Interview answer:** "Operational Apps are action-oriented — they help employees handle specific tasks like checking duplicate invoices in real time"

---

## What is a View?

A **View** is the dashboard or page inside a Celonis App that visualizes the process data.

- Think of an App as a **book** and a View as a **chapter** inside it
- Each View is made up of different **components** (charts, tables, KPIs, Process Explorer)
- Views can have multiple **tabs** to organize different aspects of the process

**Example:** In an Order Management App, one View might show delivery performance, another might show order bottlenecks — each as a separate tab.

---

## Components Inside a View

When you open a View, you will typically see:

| Component             | What It Does                                         |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| **KPI List**          | Shows the most important process metrics as numbers  |
| **Charts**            | Visual representation of data (bar, pie, line charts)|
| **Tables**            | Detailed rows and columns of process data            |
| **Process Explorer**  | Visual map of how the process actually flows         |
| **Variant Explorer**  | Shows different paths/variations the process takes   |

> Note: Some components may not appear in every View — Data Analysts customize each View based on business needs.

---

## Types of Charts and Tables in Views

### Category Charts
- Used to show **categorical information**
- Allow easy comparisons and pattern identification
- Examples: **Bar charts, Pie charts, Donut charts**
- Use case: "Which product category has the most delayed deliveries?"

### Time-Series Charts
- Used to show **trends over time**
- Examples: Line charts, Area charts
- Use case: "How has our on-time delivery rate changed over the last 6 months?"

### Tables
- Show detailed data in rows and columns
- Allow drill-down into specific cases
- Use case: "Show me all invoices that were paid late this month"

---

## What are Process Metrics?

Process Metrics are **quantitative measures** used to evaluate the efficiency and quality of a business process.

In simple terms: **Process Metrics = KPIs of a business process**

They can appear in Views as:
- A standalone number (e.g., "Average delivery time: 8.53 days")
- Inside charts or tables

### Examples of Process Metrics by Business Process:

| Process              | Sample Metrics                                     |
|----------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Order Management     | On-Time Delivery Rate, Cycle Time, Net Order Value |
| Accounts Payable     | Duplicate Invoice Rate, Payment Terms Compliance   |
| Accounts Receivable  | Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)                       |
| Procurement          | Purchase Order Cycle Time                          |
| Inventory Management | Stock Turnover Rate                                |

---

## What is a Dimension?

A **Dimension** is a category of attributes used to break down or filter process metrics.

- Think of Dimension as the **"group by"** in a data analysis
- It helps you analyze metrics from different angles

**Examples of Dimensions:**
- Customer Name
- Vendor Name
- Sales Organization
- Region
- Material Group

**Interview example answer:**
> "If I want to see Net Order Value broken down by Material Group, then Material Group is my Dimension. It helps me identify which category of materials is contributing most to delays or revenue."

> **Important:** Process Metrics and Dimensions are different things — Metrics are numbers (what you measure), Dimensions are categories (how you slice the data).

---

## More on Views — Navigation Features

Inside a View, you will find interactive elements to help you explore data:

- **Tabs** — Navigate between different sections of a View (e.g., Process Discovery tab, Charts & Tables tab)
- **Filters** — Narrow down data to specific time periods, regions, or categories
- **Drill-down** — Click into a chart to see more detailed data underneath

## My Real-World Example

Imagine Flipkart's order delivery process:

- **App:** Order Management App
- **View:** Delivery Performance Dashboard
- **KPI (Process Metric):** On-Time Delivery Rate = 64.4%
- **Dimension:** Region → South India shows only 55% on-time

This is exactly, how companies use Celonis to move from assumptions to facts.
