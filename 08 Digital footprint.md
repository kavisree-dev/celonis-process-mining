DIGITAL FOOTPRINT:

**Introduction:**

**Every action performed in digital systems creates data.
This data is called a digital footprint.**

In Process Mining, digital footprints are extremely important because they provide the raw information needed to analyze business processes.

Without **digital** **footprints**, Process Mining **cannot** **work**.

---

**What is a Digital Footprint?**

A digital footprint is the trail of data created when people or systems perform activities online or inside software platforms.

It is the record of what happened, when it happened, and who performed it.

**Digital Footprint = digital activity record**

---

**Examples in Daily Life:**

- searching on Google
- online shopping
- sending emails
- using social media
- logging into college portals
- making online payments

Each activity creates data that becomes part of digital footprint.

---

**Two Types of Digital Footprint:**

**1**. Active Digital Footprint:

This is data you intentionally create.

Examples:

- posting on LinkedIn
- filling forms
- uploading files
- sending emails

---

**2**. Passive Digital Footprint:

This is data collected automatically.

Examples:

- browsing history
- cookies
- login timestamps
- system tracking
- location tracking

Even if users do not actively create it, systems still record it.

---

**Why It Matters in Process Mining?**

Process Mining uses these digital footprints to:

- discover process flows
- identify bottlenecks
- detect delays
- analyze compliance
- improve operations

No digital footprint means no process visibility.

That is why system data is the foundation of Process Mining.

---

**A digital footprint is the data trail created by activities in digital systems, and it forms the foundation of Process Mining.**
